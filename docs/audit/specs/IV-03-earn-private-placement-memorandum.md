**6.2 BurnerBonds: Economics.** A BurnerBond is a zero-coupon secured note. It is never written down to absorb a credit loss and is paid from the BondVault ahead of every loss layer, but any amount the BondVault does not pay is an unsecured claim ranking equally with Lending Co.'s other general unsecured creditors (Section 7.3, step 6). The price per one thousand dollars ($1,000) of Maturity Amount is $1,000 divided by (1 + Issue Yield) raised to the term in years. The Issue Yield is fixed when you buy. Bonds with the same issue and maturity dates form a "**Series**", which is a Bond Class under L-01. All Series rank equally. A bond's "**Accreted Value**" rises steadily from its price to its Maturity Amount.

**6.3 BurnerBonds: The Ladder and Pricing.** During an Offering Period, Lending Co. publishes on the Earn page a "**Ladder**" of terms, each with its Issue Yield, price and Maturity Date. A longer term offered on the same day never has a lower Issue Yield than a shorter one.

Lending Co. may update the Ladder, withdraw a term or stop sales at any time. An update applies only to purchases and rolls priced after it.

## Part 7. Capital Structure and Loss Order

**7.1 Three Classes.** Lending Co. has three classes of claims: the Pool Class (all Pool Shares), a Bond Class for each BurnerBond Issuance, and the Residual Class held by Holdings. They are contractual classes of one company, not separate series.

**7.2 Residual Capital.** "**Residual Capital**" is Holdings' contributed capital and retained earnings in Lending Co., held in Liquid Assets, other than the Loss Reserve. At the Memorandum Date it is [●]. Holdings is never required to contribute more, gives no guarantee and has no liability to Earn Holders.

**7.3 Loss Order.** Every credit loss on Lending Co.'s book, net of recoveries and collateral, is absorbed in this order. Each layer is exhausted before the next bears anything:

1. **Spread** for the period in which the loss is recognized (carry and fees earned, less the cost of funds);
2. **Lending Co.'s own Loss Reserve**, all of it;
3. **Credit Backstop Draws** from Clear's AssurancePool under I-11, for charged-off principal only, up to Available Coverage and never beyond it. Each Draw is a limited-recourse advance that Lending Co. repays only from later Spread and recoveries (Part 8);
4. **Residual Capital**;
5. **the Pool Class**, by Pool Loss Charges applied first to open Loss Holdbacks and then pro rata among all Pool Shares, reducing their Redemption Amounts; and
6. **BurnerBonds, never by write-down.** BurnerBond holders bear a loss only if every layer above is exhausted, the BondVault and Redemption Reserve have been applied to them, and Lending Co.'s remaining assets cannot pay a Maturity Amount when due. They then bear the shortfall as unpaid creditors, ratably by accreted value.

**Pool Shares absorb losses on the whole credit book, including credit funded with BurnerBond proceeds.** Recoveries restore the layers in reverse: the Pool Class first, then Residual Capital, then the Credit Backstop until every advance is repaid, then the Loss Reserve.

**7.4 Secured Credit.** A loss on savings-backed or asset-backed credit is met first from the Member's pledged collateral. Only what the collateral does not cover is a credit loss.

**7.5 Payment Order.** Each month, and whenever amounts are due, Lending Co. applies its funds in this order, each step paid in full before the next: (1) Operating Expenses up to one hundred ten percent (110%) of budget; (2) BurnerBond Maturity Amounts due, and amounts payable on a bond before maturity under Section 4.9 or 4.10 of IV-01, to the extent not paid from the BondVault or applied to a pledgor's secured debt; (3) Pool Share redemptions in queue order, up to available liquidity; (4) reserves, in the order Redemption Reserve, Savings-Backed Margin, thirty (30) day liquidity coverage, then the Timing Buffer; (5) the Credit Enhancement Fee under I-11, and then, in any month in which a Credit Backstop advance is outstanding and the Timing Buffer is met, all retained Spread then remaining, up to the advances outstanding, as a Spread Repayment into the Credit Backstop, each from retained Spread; (6) Operating Expenses above the Step 1 limit; and (7) the balance to Holdings as a distribution, or retained.

**7.6 Reserves.** Lending Co. must at all times hold: a Timing Buffer of twelve (12) months of Expected Credit Loss in its Loss Reserve (using loss-rate floors of fifteen percent (15%) a year for income-backed credit and Clear Boost, and ten percent (10%) for Partner Credit and Clear Cash, until it has its own data); the Redemption Reserve; the Savings-Backed Margin; and thirty (30) days of projected net cash outflows. Each is reported separately and never combined into one ratio. The AssurancePool never counts toward any of them.

**7.6A Equity Cushion.** Holdings' equity in Lending Co., being Residual Capital plus the Loss Reserve, must be at least ten percent (10%) of the Pool Shares (at their Redemption Amounts) and BurnerBonds (at Accreted Value) outstanding (L-01, Section 8.6A). While it is below that level, Lending Co. sells no new Earn Product other than BurnerBonds rolling under a Roll Confirmation, makes no distribution to Holdings, and the Manager proposes a cure. Holdings is never required to contribute capital to restore it.

**7.7 Size Discipline.** If the ratio of credit revenue to net charge-offs on the Unsecured Book (the Coverage Multiple) falls below 2.0, Holdings' capital must cover the gap described in L-01, Section 8.6, and the Unsecured Book may not grow until it does. Below 1.5, or with annualized net charge-offs of sixteen percent (16%) or more, Lending Co. stops new unsecured credit.

**7.8 When a Reserve Is Short.** If any reserve requirement is not met, all Spread is retained, no distribution is made to Holdings, the Manager's markup is deferred, no capital is deployed into new credit except existing commitments (unless the Lending Board decides limited origination speeds the cure), and no new Earn Product is issued except BurnerBonds rolling under a Roll Confirmation. Pool redemptions continue.

**7.9 Distributions to Holdings.** Lending Co. may pay Holdings only from Step 7, and never while a reserve is short, the Coverage Multiple is below 2.0, any Maturity Amount or due Pool redemption is unpaid, or a licence requirement would be breached. While a Credit Backstop advance is outstanding, Step 5 takes all Spread that remains after reserves, so none of it reaches Step 7 until the advances are repaid.

**7.10 On Winding Up.** Lending Co. keeps servicing its loans and makes no new ones. Assets go, in order: the BondVault to BurnerBonds; Member collateral to its debt; costs and creditors the law ranks first; general creditors, including BurnerBonds for any balance; the Pool Class, up to the Redemption Amounts of Pool Shares and any open Loss Holdbacks; then Holdings.

## Part 8. The Credit Backstop

**8.1 What It Is.** Clear owns a reserve called the AssurancePool, held in smart contracts under Clear's two-person control and operated by Clear Financial. It has two segregated compartments. The **Credit Backstop** compartment makes limited-recourse advances to Lending Co. for charged-off principal under I-11. The **Member Assurance** compartment funds Member protections under MB-10 and never pays a credit loss. The two never commingle.

**8.2 What It Covers.** After Spread and the whole Loss Reserve have been applied to a month's credit losses on the Covered Book (Lending Co.'s unsecured credit to Members), Lending Co. may draw the remaining charged-off principal from the Credit Backstop, as an advance, up to "**Available Coverage**": the least of (a) the compartment's balance; (b) twelve (12) months of Expected Credit Loss on the Covered Book and, from the Charge-Off History Date (Section 8.5), the lesser of that amount and twenty-five percent (25%) of the principal charged off on the Covered Book in the last twelve (12) months; and (c) the amount that, with twelve (12) months of fees on it, equals ten percent (10%) of Clear's consolidated total assets. It never covers carry, losses on credit to affiliates (including I-06), losses from lending in breach of law or the Credit Policy, losses caused by fraud or gross negligence of a Clear company, refunds, fines or legal costs, or losses on loan sales.

**8.3 How It Is Funded.** Lending Co. pays Clear a Credit Enhancement Fee, a commitment fee of eight percent (8%) a year on average daily Available Coverage, from retained Spread. Clear's policy is to contribute twenty-five percent (25%) of card interchange, plus any revenue its Board allocates. Inflows fill the Credit Backstop first, up to a floor equal to the greater of Lending Co.'s Timing Buffer shortfall and five percent (5%) of the Covered Book; above it they split by a Board-set annual allocation (50/50 in year one). Money leaves the Credit Backstop for Member Assurance only if the floor is still met, with Board approval, ten (10) Business Days' notice to Lending Co. and two-person control. No bond, Pool, ESA or collateral money ever enters the AssurancePool, and no redemption path draws on it.

**8.4 Its Limits.** **The Credit Backstop is not a guarantee, not insurance and not a promise by Clear or its Members.** Clear pays only from the Credit Backstop compartment and only up to Available Coverage. Clear need not hold any balance, contribute anything or refill it after a draw. Lending Co. repays each advance only as Section 8.7 describes. Clear or Lending Co. may end I-11 on one hundred eighty (180) days' notice, and it ends automatically if Clear stops controlling Holdings or Holdings stops controlling Lending Co. On termination, coverage ends for losses charged off afterwards. **You have no rights under I-11 and cannot enforce it.** Lending Co. never counts the Credit Backstop toward its reserves and sizes its book as if it did not exist.

**8.5 Size at the Memorandum Date.** The Backstop Balance is [●] and the Backstop Floor is [●]. Because it is funded mainly by interchange and fees that grow with activity, the Credit Backstop is expected to be small in the early Phases, and may be zero. Until Lending Co. has twelve (12) months of charge-off history on the Covered Book, limb (b) of Section 8.2 is twelve (12) months of Expected Credit Loss; from the "**Charge-Off History Date**" (the last day of the twelfth (12th) full month after the month of Lending Co.'s first charge-off on the Covered Book) the trailing charge-off measure also applies.

**8.6 Reports.** At least quarterly, Lending Co. reports to Earn Holders the Backstop Balance, Backstop Floor, Available Coverage, Draws in the quarter, advances outstanding, and whether the floor was met at each month end.

**8.7 Advances and Repayment.** Each Draw is a limited-recourse advance by Clear to Lending Co., not insurance or an indemnity. It bears no interest and has no maturity. Lending Co. repays it only from (a) recoveries on charged-off loans, after the Pool Class and Residual Capital have been restored, and (b) in each month in which the Timing Buffer is met, all retained Spread left at Step 5 after the Credit Enhancement Fee. Repayments go back into the Credit Backstop. Clear has no other recourse to Lending Co., and an advance is never repaid from the BondVault, the Loss Reserve, Pool Assets or Member collateral. Because repayments come at Step 5, after BurnerBond payments, Pool interest and redemptions and every reserve, they never reduce what is paid to you, but they come before any distribution to Holdings. An advance not repaid is forgiven only when Lending Co. is wound up, or after I-11 ends, once recoveries on the related loans have run off. Lending Co. records each advance as a liability until it is repaid or forgiven.

## Part 9. Risk Factors

An Earn Product is speculative and illiquid. You could lose all of it. The risks below are the ones Lending Co. considers material; others may exist.

### Credit and business risks

**9.1 Credit losses.** Lending Co. lends unsecured to Members on bank-data underwriting, without credit reports, many of whom have thin or damaged credit files. Loss rates may be far higher than the floors in the Credit Policy. Pool Shares absorb losses after thin layers of Spread, Loss Reserve, Credit Backstop and Residual Capital, all of which may be small or zero in the early Phases.

**9.2 Capped pricing.** The CFL caps what Lending Co. can charge, and Lending Co. charges no fees. It cannot raise prices to cover higher losses. Spread may be too small to absorb losses or pay expenses.

**9.3 Early-stage company.** Lending Co. has no loans, revenue, track record or audited financial statements. Clear and its companies are newly formed, with a small team and limited capital, and their model is untested.

**9.4 Negative carry.** BurnerBond yields are fixed when issued. In Phase 0, and whenever capital sits idle, Lending Co. may earn less on Liquid Assets than it owes on BurnerBonds. That shortfall is borne by Residual Capital and then by Pool Shares.

**9.5 Concentration.** Nearly all borrowers are expected to live in the Inland Empire of Southern California. A regional downturn, a local employer closing, a natural disaster or wildfire could raise losses across the book at once. Partner Credit is concentrated in a small number of local merchants.

**9.6 Merchant and fraud risk.** Lending Co. funds merchant payouts whether or not the Member repays, and bears identity fraud and Member fraud. Collusion between a merchant and Members could create losses before controls detect it.

**9.7 Bank and vendor dependence.** Lending Co. depends on Clear Financial and its program vendors: Lithic (card and ACH program management), Bridge (dollar and USDC conversion), the Bank Partner, [●], Privy (smart accounts) and Plaid (bank data). If any of them stops serving, changes terms or fails, Lending Co. may be unable to lend, collect or pay you on time. A backup servicer is engaged under I-07, and after a servicer default BurnerBond holders of a majority of the Accreted Value may require the move to it, but transfer would take time.

**9.8 Key-person risk.** The Founder is Clear's sole director and sole officer during the Founding Period and is expected to be Lending Co.'s chief executive officer. Lending Co. depends heavily on the Founder, whose death, disability or departure would seriously disrupt the business. There is no key-person insurance.

### Liquidity risks

**9.9 No secondary market.** Earn Products are restricted securities, cannot be transferred except as Section 6.10 permits, and will not trade anywhere. You may not be able to get your money out when you want.

**9.10 Pool queues and valuation.** Pool redemptions are paid only from Vault cash. When most of the pool is lent, your request may wait months for collections, and you bear losses while you wait, because you are paid at the Share Value when filled. When expected losses rise, part of each redemption is held back for as long as the Pool Loss Allowance stays posted, which could be a long time, and the held-back amount earns nothing and can be lost to realized losses. A Member who subscribes while an allowance is posted pays the full Share Value and shares in losses that were already expected. The allowance depends on estimates. Redemptions can be suspended for the events in Section 6.8.

**9.11 Bond maturity and rate risk.** BurnerBonds have no early exit and terms up to thirty-six (36) months (sixty (60) months from Phase 3), while the loans they fund are short. The Redemption Reserve covers only part of outstanding bonds until ninety (90) days before maturity. If many bonds mature together and loans cannot be collected fast enough, payment could be late or incomplete.

**9.12 Limited recourse.** Pool Shares are payable only from the Pool Assets, and a payment default gives you a claim only against them. BurnerBonds' security covers only the BondVault and Redemption Reserve. No other Clear company, and no Member, is liable to you.

### Legal and regulatory risks

**9.13 CFL licensing.** Lending Co.'s business depends on its CFL licence. The Department of Financial Protection and Innovation could suspend, condition or revoke it, or find that pricing, disclosures or fee practices breach the CFL. Remediation, refunds and penalties would reduce assets available to you. Loss of the licence is not an event of default on the BurnerBonds, so it would not by itself let holders accelerate; instead Lending Co. would stop selling and rolling BurnerBonds and tell you within one (1) Business Day, and your bonds would be paid at maturity from its remaining assets.

**9.14 Consumer credit law.** Lending Co. is subject to Regulations Z, E and B, the Fair Credit Reporting Act, the Rosenthal Act and California's Consumer Financial Protection Law. The overdraft-line structure of the spend card, automatic clearing from deposits and on-chain liquidation of collateral are novel and could be challenged.

**9.15 Military Lending Act and SCRA.** Credit to covered servicemembers is capped at a thirty-six percent (36%) military APR, cannot include arbitration, and may restrict pledges of collateral. Violations can make a loan void.

**9.16 True-lender and program structure.** Clear runs the merchant program, Clear Financial provides technology and servicing for a Program Services Fee that is not paid per loan, and Lending Co. lends and receives the merchant's Program Fee as its own. A regulator or court could still find that Clear or Clear Financial is the true lender, an unlicensed broker or an unlicensed servicer or collector, or that a merchant fee is a charge received in advance, which could make loans unenforceable or require refunds.

**9.17 Digital assets: DFAL and the GENIUS Act.** Clear Financial's handling of CLRUSD and USDC is under review under California's Digital Financial Assets Law, whose licensing requirement took effect in July 2026. CLRUSD may also be treated as a payment stablecoin under the federal GENIUS Act, whose prohibitions take effect no later than January 2027. Clear Financial relies for now on a small-volume exemption for its California activity and plans to obtain a licence before that activity reaches the exemption's limit. CLRUSD Send between Members is on from launch, which makes the payment stablecoin risk greater. If a licence is needed and not obtained, savings-backed credit, Earn payments in USDC and the ESA could be restricted, and Lending Co.'s business could shrink sharply.

**9.18 Securities law.** Each Earn Offering must meet its own exemption. If Lending Co. failed to take reasonable steps to verify a purchaser in the Rule 506(c) Offering, or a purchaser in the Rule 506(b) Offering had been reached through general solicitation, or a purchaser in the Intrastate Offering was not a California resident, or the offerings were found to be one integrated offering, the exemption could be lost. Because the Rule 506(c) Offering permits general solicitation, a lost exemption there would probably have no fallback. Purchasers could then have rescission rights, and Lending Co. could be unable to raise further capital. The Intrastate Offering and any later Regulation A offering depend on permits and qualifications that may never be obtained. Lending Co. relies on the exclusion in Section 3(c)(4) of the Investment Company Act of 1940 for a company substantially all of whose business is confined to making small loans (L-01, Section 2.9). To stay within it, at least eighty-five percent (85%) of its non-cash assets and of its income must come from consumer loans to natural persons, which may require it to pause Pool Share subscriptions or limit other activities. Any Interim Tranche it holds under I-06 counts against the remaining fifteen percent (15%); the Lending Sub, which makes all other I-06 credit, is owned by Holdings and is outside Lending Co. If it lost that exclusion, it could not continue as structured.

**9.19 Changes in law.** Law on consumer credit, fintech, digital assets and private offerings is changing fast. New rules could raise costs, restrict products or end the business.

### Technology risks

**9.20 Smart contracts.** Earn Products, the BondVault, the Yield Pool, the pledge module and the AssurancePool run on smart contracts. Despite audits, a bug or exploit could cause loss of funds or wrong payments. Upgrades go through a timelock of at least seven (7) days, but an exploit can happen faster than any pause.

**9.21 Custody.** Your tokens sit in your own smart account. If you lose access to it or it is compromised, Lending Co. may be unable to recover your position. USDC held by Lending Co. depends on Circle's reserves and redemption; if USDC loses its dollar value, Lending Co.'s assets and payments to you fall with it.

**9.22 Oracles and attestations.** Some figures, including fiat receipts, Members' states of residence and the monthly Covered Book used by the Credit Backstop, are written on-chain by attestation. A wrong attestation could misstate limits, floors or coverage.

**9.23 Network risk.** Congestion, a chain halt or a fork could delay or prevent payments.

**9.24 Cybersecurity.** Clear handles sensitive personal and financial data. A breach could cause losses, fines, litigation and loss of Members.

### Structural and conflict risks

**9.25 Related-party conflicts.** Lending Co. is controlled by the Founder through Clear and Holdings, buys services from affiliates at cost plus a markup, and pays Clear for the Credit Backstop. Decisions may favor Clear or its Members over Earn Holders. See Part 10.

**9.26 Members on both sides.** Earn Holders are paid from carry charged to other Members. Clear's purpose is to keep credit affordable for Members, and Lending Co. may choose lower prices, more forgiving hardship terms or slower collections than a lender focused on investors.

**9.27 The Credit Backstop may not be there.** It may be small, exhausted or terminated. Before the Charge-Off History Date its cap rests on modeled Expected Credit Loss, not on any loss history; from that date the cap also depends on actual charge-offs and may be lower. It covers principal only. Clear may move money above its floor to Member Assurance. Each Draw is an advance that Lending Co. must repay from later Spread and recoveries, so while advances are outstanding that Spread goes to Clear rather than rebuilding Residual Capital, the layer that stands ahead of the Pool Class.

**9.28 Tax risk.** The tax treatment of Earn Products is uncertain. Pool Shares are drafted as debt, with a principal-plus-interest claim, a payment-default remedy and an equity cushion, but the Internal Revenue Service could still treat them as equity, as Part 13 explains. You may owe tax on BurnerBond discount before you receive cash.

**9.29 Dilution.** Later BurnerBonds share the BondVault equally with yours, and later Pool Shares share the pool.

**9.30 Forward-looking statements.** This Memorandum contains statements about plans, Phases, growth, losses and returns. They are estimates, not promises, and actual results may differ materially. Lending Co. has no duty to update them except as Part 19 provides.

## Part 13. Tax Considerations

This is a general summary of U.S. federal and California income tax for individual U.S. holders. Consult your own tax adviser.

**13.1 Lending Co.'s Status.** Lending Co. is disregarded as separate from Holdings, a C corporation, so long as no Earn Product is treated as equity. Its income is taxed to Holdings.

**13.2 BurnerBonds.** A BurnerBond is a zero-coupon debt instrument, and the difference between the issue price and the Maturity Amount is original issue discount ("**OID**"). For a bond maturing more than one (1) year after issue, you must include OID in income each year as it accrues on a constant-yield basis, **even though you receive no cash until maturity**. For a twelve (12) month bond, a short-term obligation, a cash-method individual generally reports the discount as interest at maturity unless electing to accrue it. OID is ordinary interest income, reported on Form 1099-OID (longer bonds) or Form 1099-INT (twelve-month bonds). Each roll is a payment at maturity and a new purchase. Payment of a pledged bond before maturity under Section 4.9 of IV-01 is a retirement of the bond, even though the amount is applied to your debt.

**13.3 Pool Shares.** Lending Co. treats Pool Shares as debt: a principal amount plus interest, payable on demand. It reports the Pool Interest credited to your Pool Shares as interest accruing daily, on Form 1099-INT each year whether or not you redeem. A realized Pool Loss Charge, including one applied to your Loss Holdback, is a loss of principal, not negative interest; a Pool Loss Allowance and a Loss Holdback are not losses until a charge applies them. Because the return is variable, Pool Shares might instead be contingent payment debt instruments, with different accrual rules. If the IRS treated Pool Shares as equity, Lending Co. could become a partnership for tax purposes; you could receive a Schedule K-1, be taxed on income you did not receive, and face different rules on losses. A loss of principal may be deductible only when the Pool Share is redeemed or becomes worthless, and only as a capital loss or nonbusiness bad debt.

**13.4 State Tax.** California taxes interest and OID as ordinary income and has no exemption for them. Other states may also tax them.

**13.5 Collateral and Payments.** Applying a pledged Earn Product to a debt is a disposition of it. Payment in USDC is valued at the dollar amount paid.

**13.6 Withholding.** You must give a Form W-9. Without it, Lending Co. must apply backup withholding.
