# What shares are

What a contributor holds after contributing property, and how it differs from the share tokens of
the Deed track. Nothing here is an offer or tax or legal advice; see [Contributors](./README.md).

## Shares in Clear Properties

**A contributor receives shares in Clear Properties, the company that holds contributed property.**

- **Issued by value.** Property comes in at appraised value, and shares are issued one for each
  dollar of it. A property appraised at $437,000.00 becomes 437,000 shares.
- **Nothing settled in cash.** No cash is paid either way to balance the exchange.
- **One pool.** Shares are in a single pool of contributed property, not in the contributor's own
  building. Clear Capital's own position is in the same pool.
- **The same terms for every holder.** No preferred return and no priority for anyone, Clear Capital
  included. Nobody is paid before another holder.
- **Pro rata.** Whatever is distributed is shared in proportion to shares held. See
  [What contributors receive](./what-contributors-receive.md).

As more property comes in, Clear Capital's share of the pool gets smaller.

## The downsides

Contributing rather than selling has real costs. The list is the same for one property or a
portfolio.

| | The cost |
|---|---|
| Liquidity | Shares are illiquid compared with cash |
| Control | The contributor does not control the properties |
| Paperwork | Holding shares brings its own tax paperwork. Ask your own adviser which forms apply |
| Tax | How a contribution is taxed, now and if the shares are ever sold, depends on the contributor's situation. Take it to your own tax adviser |
| Concentration | Risk moves from one building to a pool. That is a change, not a removal |

For larger contributions there is one more: early properties sit in a single corridor by design, and
anyone assessing a contribution should take that concentration into account.

## Not the same as fraction tokens

**Shares in Clear Properties are not tokens, and no contract issues or records them.**

The Deed track does have a way to split ownership of one asset into shares: fractionalizing, which
issues a share token for each asset it locks. It is a different thing:

| | Contributor shares | Deed track fraction tokens |
|---|---|---|
| What they are a share of | A pool of many contributed properties | One locked T-Deed, or one subdivided unit |
| How many there are | One per dollar of appraised value | Whatever number the asset's owner sets; nothing values the asset |
| Who issues them | Clear Properties, off the chain | The person who locked the asset |
| Payments to holders | Distributed pro rata | None: fraction tokens carry no payments |
| Transfer | Illiquid | Transferable tokens, with an optional limit per wallet |

How fractionalizing and subdividing work is in
[Deeds and real-world assets](../overview/deeds-and-real-world-assets.md), with the full account in
[Fractionalization](../protocol/deed-track/fractionalization.md) and
[Subdivision](../protocol/deed-track/subdivision.md).
