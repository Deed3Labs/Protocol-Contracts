Payouts.

What the co-op owes, what the shop already holds, and the difference between them — with the withdraw flow that has to ask where money is coming from.

Two balances, not one

  • Owed and held are different kinds of money. What the co-op owes is a claim — scheduled, and released only as far as the payout pool allows. What sits in the cash account is already the merchant’s, movable at any hour. One figure would misstate both.
  • A business asks a different question than a person. Not “what have I got” but “how much can I move right now, and when does the rest land” — so that is the figure at the top and the date is the subline.
  • Real routing and account numbers. A merchant who cannot copy them cannot set up a transfer from their own bank. This is business banking, not a wallet.

The cycle card’s proportions, with the bar it needs: no header, one line with the cycle figure and the countdown, the track under it, and one line in the footer with the action. The track is the useful part — how much of this cycle has already released is exactly what a merchant is counting down to, and the caption names both halves. The border carries the state — settled when something is free today, underway when the pool has nothing free yet, cobalt on the day it pays out.

Processing is the only place the hops are visible, and it is three steps when the money starts as owed, two when it starts in the cash account — the shorter flow is finished rather than truncated. The done state reports both balances, because a merchant who has just moved money between pots wants to see where both stand.

Carried over from the reference

  • “Still lent out” stays gone. That money is not on loan — it is owed, and beyond what the pool can free today. Releases Oct 14 answers with a date instead of borrowing the yield pool’s vocabulary for something unrelated.
  • The three lines are parallel — states of the same money rather than one account and two conditions.

Open

  • The plan rate. Card processing is 2.7% + 35¢ on pay-as-you-go. A paid plan brings Clear’s part to 20–25¢; the exact figure is still open.
  • What the shop is carrying. The Oct 14 cell says $1,180.00 clears first at no carry, which means a merchant can owe Clear — but nothing on this page says how that balance arose or where a merchant goes to see it.
  • Who can withdraw. The role list says a manager can send payouts to the business bank, so a manager’s version of this page should exist: the figures and Chase as a destination, but not the debit card and not the account details.
  • Statements. The history footer points at them and they do not exist yet. A shop’s accountant will want a month, not three rows.
