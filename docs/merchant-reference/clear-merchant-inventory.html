Inventory.

What the shop sells, what it costs, and how many are on the shelf. It exists so the counter can charge from a list instead of typing a figure, and so an owner finds out a tire is running low before a customer does.

What it is for

  • A charge can be built from items. The amount screen stays, but a writer can tap two tires and two mounts instead of adding them up. The price comes from here, so it is the same for every writer.
  • Stock is a count, not bookkeeping. It answers “do we have it” and “when do we reorder”, nothing more. Cost is optional and only an owner sees it.
  • Stock follows the charge, not the tap. Raising a charge holds the items. Approval takes them off the shelf. If the charge expires or is cancelled, they go back. So the shelf count is never wrong because a customer walked away.
  • Services keep no stock. Mount and balance, alignment and labour have a price and nothing to count, and they are not taxed.
  • Nothing is deleted once it has been sold. An item with charges against it is archived, so every past charge still says what it was for.

Inventory · owner · tablet

The top is the Raised today component, counting items instead of money: 8 stocked items in one bar, in stock, low and out, with the key as a thin footer. The owner’s note is the shelf’s value at cost. Below it, the items grouped by category, each group saying how many need stock. A row is the item, its stock and its price, in fixed columns so prices line up. Low is amber and says how many are left; out is red. Stock held for a waiting charge is named in the row, so 6 left with 2 held reads as exactly that.

An item, opened

Two cells: stock and price. Stock leads with the count on the shelf and says plainly what is free, because 6 on the shelf with 2 held for Nina’s waiting charge means 4 can be sold. The track under it shows the same thing against the reorder line: held hatched, free solid, and the line at 8 the shop set. Recent is the stock’s own history, every change with who or what made it: a sale names the customer and the writer, a delivery says how many came in, a count says what it was before. The one rule about prices sits in the footer: a new price does not touch a charge already raised.

Options on an item

Choices that change what is sold, and what it costs. A tire can carry a road hazard warranty and extras; a taco can need a protein. Options sit in groups, and each group says two things: whether the customer must pick, and whether they pick one or any. Reached from the Options strip at the foot of the item’s price, or from Edit item. The left side is the list an owner edits, in the order the counter will see it; the right side is that counter’s sheet, drawn live, so the owner sees exactly what Jen will tap.

Sales tax on an item

Each item says how it is taxed, in four plain kinds: taxable goods, labour not taxed, prepared food, and exempt. The rate itself comes from the shop’s address, worked out in Settings, so an item never carries a percentage of its own. Adding and editing items now end with the choice, and the item page shows it beside the size.

Tiles, for quick orders

A shop picks whether the counter sees its items as a list or tiles, in Settings. Both are drawn in New Charge, under From items, with the food truck that tiles are really for.

A charge, from items

This is the reason inventory exists: the counter picks instead of typing. The picker, options, quick sale and the cart are drawn in New Charge, under From items, so there is one source for every charge screen.

Inventory · counter shift · tablet

The same list, read-only. The summary keeps its bar but the note says prices and stock only, and there is no Add item. A writer can open any row to see what goes with it and start a charge.

Open

  • Where sales tax lives. The ticket taxes parts at 7.75% and not labour. The rate should be a shop setting, in Settings under Shop, and it is not drawn yet.
  • Suppliers and purchase orders. Mark reordered notes what is coming and from whom, and receiving closes it. Sending the order to the distributor from here is a bigger feature and may not belong in a counter app.
  • Variants. The same tire in six sizes is still six items: options change what is sold, not which stock it comes from. A shop with a large catalogue will want sizes that each keep their own stock.
  • Order names and numbers. The food truck’s order 47 for Sam implies a call-out screen and a setting to name orders. Neither is drawn yet.
  • Who can change stock. This draws managers and owners. A shop may want counter staff to receive deliveries.
  • Stock alerts in Settings. The Running low card implies a notification. Notifications in Settings needs a Stock running low switch, on for owners by default.
  • The nav. Inventory is added between Charges and Payouts, and on the phone as a box icon. The other merchant files still show five items.
