Charges.

The list a writer opens to answer two questions — who has not confirmed, and what did we run — a charge opened, and the refund that needs two people.

What the list is for

  • Two questions, one screen. Who has not confirmed, and what did we run. Waiting sorts to the top whatever time it was raised, because those are the only rows with anything to do.
  • Every row names who raised it. Not surveillance — it is how an owner works out which writer is actually offering it, and who needs another fifteen minutes.
  • Expired is a state, not a disappearance. A charge that timed out is a lost sale, and a shop that can see it can chase it.

Void, and adjusting a tip

A void is a same-day undo: the card is never charged, cash comes back from the drawer, everything goes back in stock, and there is no fee. It needs a manager’s PIN, and after the card batch settles it is gone and only a refund remains. A tip can change until the day is closed, for a tip written on a printed slip after the card was taken.

The record keeps both names. An owner reviewing the month needs to know who asked as well as who approved.

The owner sees a different number from the writer, and that is the point. Jen sees what Marcus gets back. Mike sees what it does to his payout — $3,815.15 instead of $4,218.91 — which is the figure the person authorising actually decides on. Nothing is said to the customer until step three completes, so a refund a writer asked for and an owner declined never became a promise.

Escalate by size, as retail already does, and the owner sets the line: under their figure a manager PIN clears it at the till, above it approval comes from the owner’s phone. Set to Off, every refund waits. The code authorises one displayed action and expires with it — it is never a session and never an owner sign-in.

Open

  • Partial refunds. Everything here refunds the whole charge. A returned part on a $412 ticket is the common case in a tyre shop, and it changes the customer’s plan rather than closing it.
  • Refunding an expired charge. Nothing moved, so there is nothing to refund — but a writer may well try. Tom B.’s row should open something that says so and offers to send it again.
  • Where a dispute goes. A customer who disputes a charge with Clear is the merchant-side mirror of the member app’s dispute page, and it does not exist here yet.
