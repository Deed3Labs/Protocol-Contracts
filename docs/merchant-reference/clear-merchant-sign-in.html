Signing in.

Three levels of authority, and only one of them is a login. The device is enrolled once by the owner; the shift is a name and a PIN, so every charge says who raised it; and anything that moves money needs the owner, signed in with Privy.

The three levels

A PIN is attribution, not security. Four digits on a counter will be watched and shared; what they buy is the name on every charge row. The real boundary is the enrolled device, which the owner can remove in one tap from anywhere. A lost tablet is the risk; a guessed PIN is not.

1 · Enrolling a device

Once, by the owner. The new tablet asks for the code from Settings, Devices, Add a device, or for the owner to sign in right there. Then it states the ceiling before it asks for consent: it can raise charges up to $1,500.00, enforced by policy rather than by the app, and it cannot move money. It locks after five idle minutes. The last line is the one that makes a lost tablet survivable.

4 · A forgotten PIN, and a lost tablet

A forgotten PIN is reset by a manager or the owner in Staff, with their own PIN; the person picks a new one on their next shift. A lost tablet is removed from Settings, from any device: it signs out at once, ends any shift on it, and loses nothing, because it only ever held a session. The removed tablet says what happened and how to set it up again.

How this maps to Privy organizations

The shape of the shop is the shape of a Privy organization, which moves several rules out of the app and into places a compromised app cannot reach. The cash account’s USDC sits in the same organization wallet; Bridge moves dollars in and out of it.

Open

  • Moving refund sign-offs to Privy intents. Not for v1: Clear’s own approval record works and the screens do not change. Worth it when the co-op wants to claim it cannot move shop funds alone.
  • How long the owner stays signed in. Drawn as 15 minutes. Shorter is safer on a shared counter; longer is kinder at month end in the back office.
  • Staff reaching their own charges from their phones. Not drawn. It would need a staff sign-in that is not a PIN.
