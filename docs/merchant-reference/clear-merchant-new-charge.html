New charge.

The flow a counter runs forty times a week: start with an amount or with items, check out, take the tip, then take it by Clear, card, cash or a mix, and the ways each can fail.

The shape of it

Every charge runs left to right through the same five steps, whichever way it starts and however it is paid. The sections below follow this order, with the states of each step under it.

  • Two ways in, one way through. A typed amount and a cart both arrive at Checkout. After that the flow does not care how the charge was built.
  • How they pay is asked once, at Checkout. Clear leads, because it is what the shop is here to offer; card, cash and a split are beside it, not behind a menu.
  • A flow replaces the page. Inside a charge the nav goes and a slim header takes its place: close or back, what this is, and who is on shift.
  • Every way to pay ends the same, at paid with a receipt and New charge, so a writer never has to remember which path they are on.

The words on the left, the code on the right — and both cells carry a header and a footer, so their rules meet across the seam. 5 · Paying by card

Some customers will just want to pay by card, and the counter should not have to leave the app to take it. Card is one of the ways to pay on Checkout. The counter app is the web app wrapped with Capacitor, so it drives a Stripe Reader M2 over Bluetooth, a smart reader, or Tap to Pay on a phone running the Clear app. Cards stay off until the shop connects its Stripe account in Settings.

The card screen is two halves. On the left, the charge as the cart review draws it: the amount at full size, the lines, and what makes up the total. On the right, the reader, and what it is doing. Waiting is the one live thing on the screen, so it carries the pulsing dot from the style guide, and the contactless mark, the four arcs every modern reader carries, lights from the inside out. Cobalt is kept for the step the card is on. The step strip under it is the same one as the code screen: present the card, reading, approved.

When it does not work

Three failures a counter will actually hit with Clear, and each one hands the writer the sentence to say. A declined card and a failed part of a split are drawn with their own steps above.

DeclinedNot covered

Clear could not cover this one.

Dana can see why in her app. You are never told the reason — that stays between Clear and the member.

What to tell them

“It did not go through on their side — the app will tell you why. Nothing was charged.”

Expired24 hours

Dana did not approve in time.

Charges expire after 24 hours. Nothing was charged and nothing is owed. The usual cause is a phone left face-down.

What to tell them

“I will send it again — it just needs approving on your phone.”

No connectionOffline

Clear can’t reach their phone.

There is no offline queue for Clear: a charge the ledger has not seen is a promise that may not hold. Card and cash still work, and a card is stored and sent when the connection is back, up to $500.

What to tell them

“Paying over time is down for a moment. I can take a card or cash, or we can try again in a minute.”

Decisions in here

  • The fee is an owner’s figure. The old flow showed it to every writer, which contradicted the counter role. It shows before the charge exists and again at confirmation — to whoever can see the rate.
  • The code screen is addressed to the customer. Once the tablet is turned, the writer is not the reader, so the words on it are for the person holding their phone up to it.
  • Failures carry a script. Declined never tells the merchant why; expired is one tap to resend; offline says so plainly: Clear offers no queue, and card and cash carry on.

Open

  • The pay-over-time minimum. Checkout assumes Clear sets one, drawn as $50.00, below which Clear is pay now only. The figure is Clear’s decision.
  • Tips on Clear. A tip goes with the tender, so a tip paid with Clear can be split with the rest. If that is not wanted, Clear tips would have to be paid now.
  • Who processes cards, and at what fee. The card screen assumes a connected reader and a processor. Card fees are not Clear’s rates, card money may land on a different schedule, and the Payouts page would need to show it apart from Clear payouts.
  • Who can cancel a waiting charge. Here any writer can cancel one; the staff rules say a counter role can cancel one they raised. The screen needs to know which.
  • How long a waiting charge lives. Twenty-four hours is stated on the expired sheet. An end-of-day nudge to the writer before it lapses is probably worth adding.
