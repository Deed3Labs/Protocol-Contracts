Overview.

The back office: what the month did, what is owed, what it cost, and who did it — everything an owner asks at month end and nothing a writer needs mid-shift.

What this page is

  • Everything an owner asks at month end, and nothing a writer needs mid-shift. It is the one page in the app that is about the business rather than about a charge.
  • The same three blocks. The month as the figure, the trend as the live component, one insight as the temporary slot, then the slab.
  • It answers, it does not manage. Every cell ends in a link to the page that does the work — Payouts, Charges, Staff. Nothing here is a second way to change something.

Statements, and the terms

Statements existed only as a promise until now — the payout history pointed at them and nothing was behind it. A shop’s accountant wants a month, not three rows, and they want it as a file. Each statement reconciles to the payout that followed it, which is the only property that makes it worth anything.

The terms sheet says the three Nones out loud — no monthly fee, no terminal rental, no minimum volume — because every merchant has been charged all three before and will assume them until told otherwise. The rate does not move with volume, and the sheet says why: a rate that is negotiated down for one shop is being paid for by another.

Fixed from the old reference

  • The rate contradicted its own arithmetic. Now the fee is worked out per charge: 1.25% on charges paid now and 2.0% over time, matching the charge flow and the refund maths.
  • Owed to you disagreed with Payouts. $4,218.91 here against $4,218.91 owed on the payouts page. It is $4,218.91, with the $612.40 already held named separately in the footer.
  • Luis was a counter here and a manager everywhere else. He approves refunds under $500.00 in the refund flow, so he is a manager.

Open

  • Founding-partner terms. If Mike’s Tire is one of the first five shops at 2% for life, every figure in all five files changes. It is a pricing decision, not a design one, but it has to be settled before any of these numbers are shown to a real merchant.
  • The same month last year. The fees cell has a row for it and an em dash in it, because the shop has not been on Clear a year. Worth deciding whether that row appears at all before it can be filled.
  • Who the accountant is. Send to my accountant implies an address on file, and nothing anywhere collects one.
